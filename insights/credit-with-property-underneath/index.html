Credit with property underneath.

notes from the firm
2026

A fifth of our capital is allocated to real estate credit — loans secured on property, bought at a discount when the borrower, the lender or both need a way out. It is the part of the strategy that raises the most questions, and the logic is the simplest.

Distressed credit frightens buyers whose business ends where the paperwork does. If the loan defaults and the keys change hands, a credit fund holds a building it never wanted; we hold a building — which is our ordinary business. The worst case of the credit strategy is the everyday case of the other two.

So we underwrite the asset first and the paper second. Before pricing a loan we price the collateral as if we were buying it directly: location, condition, what our team would spend, what it would let for, what it would sell for in an unremarkable year. Then we work backwards through the legal position — ranking, enforcement route, timeline, costs — and the discount does the rest. The price we pay must produce an acceptable outcome in every branch: the borrower repays, the debt is restructured, or the property becomes ours at a basis below anything available on the open market.

Special situations reward preparation more than boldness. The work is unglamorous — reading files, walking buildings, understanding exactly how long a process takes in a particular jurisdiction — and it is precisely the work a firm that builds and manages property already knows how to do.

Credit, for us, is not a separate business. It is another door into the same buildings, opened at a better price.
