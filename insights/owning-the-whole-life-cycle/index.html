Owning the whole life cycle.

notes from the firm
2026

Most of what goes wrong in real estate goes wrong at a handover. The developer hands a finished building to a landlord who did not choose its specification. The landlord hands tenants to a manager who was not in the room when the leases were written. Each specialist optimises their own stretch of the journey, and the seams between them are where value leaks out.

A.E.F. Realty is built to have no seams. The firm that signs the purchase contract is the firm that runs the works, signs the leases, manages the asset and, when the value has been built, executes the exit. One balance sheet carries the asset from the first viewing to completion of the sale. We do not manage assets for third parties, and no third party manages ours.

This changes the underwriting before it changes anything else. A buyer who will also be the operator prices what the building will actually cost to run — not what a broker's service-charge estimate says. A developer who will also be the landlord chooses specifications for how they weather, not how they photograph. A landlord who prepared the exit from the day of purchase does not discover, three weeks before a sale, that the data room should have been built two years earlier.

It also changes what we decline. An asset that only works in the hands of a particular kind of specialist operator is not for us, however attractive the entry price. We buy what we can run with our own hands, build what we know we can let, and let what we are content to hold through a bad year.

The allocation follows from the model: forty-five per cent of assets in value-add, thirty-five in ground-up development, twenty in real estate credit. Three strategies, one discipline — every position is a building we would be willing to own outright, all the way through.
