Discipline in a repricing market.

notes from the firm
2026

When debt was free, underwriting drifted into forecasting. A purchase could be justified by the rent it might achieve after the market moved, the yield it might command after rates fell, the buyer who might appear once the story was told well enough. Higher funding costs have repriced the assets; they should also reprice the habits.

Our rule is older than the cycle: the return is made at purchase. Works, letting and management convert a good basis into cash — they do not rescue a bad one. So we underwrite basis, not narrative: price per square metre against replacement cost, rebuilt yield on total capital against the financing that actually exists today, and an exit tested at the yields of a dull year, not a hot one.

In practice this means we model the asset as it stands — its current rent, its current defects, its current emptiness — and then price only the work we will do with our own team and the letting we believe our own hands can deliver. Anything that depends on the market improving is treated as upside, and upside is never paid for.

The discipline shows up mostly as absence. We pass on assets whose plan requires cap-rate compression. We pass on auctions where the winner is simply the party who assumed the most. We pass quickly, in writing, and stay on good terms — the asset that fails our underwriting this year is often sold to us, properly priced, in the next.

From a standing start, the firm has traded €7.8 million of real estate in three years on this basis. The number is small next to the institutions and that suits us: at our size, there is no pressure to deploy, and no reason to accept a purchase that only a forecast can defend.
